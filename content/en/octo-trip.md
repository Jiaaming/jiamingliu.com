## 🇨🇦Winnipeg

After a short break for a few days in early October, I spent the weekend visiting Winnipeg—a city that appears frequently in the mouths of immigration agents.

![Winnipeg is located in the geographical center of Canada and is the capital of Manitoba. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251010124810.png)

Manitoba, where Winnipeg is located, was historically one of the areas where Aboriginal people came into contact with European colonists. The city center is at the confluence of the Red River and the Assiniboine River. This land called "The Forks" has been a place of trade and gathering for various tribes for thousands of years. This is also the birthplace of the Métis people - they are the descendants of Aboriginal people and French people, and they are a very representative ethnic group in Canada's diverse narrative today.

![Red River Bank](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/5109DE06-E0EF-4876-9732-4568C554BEC9_1_105_c.jpeg)

Manitoba is also an officially bilingual region, with English and French spoken in parallel. However, the third type of writing that I often see on the streets and in museums is unexpected: I initially thought it was some ancient Aboriginal language, but it is actually a 19th-century man-made writing called Cree syllabics. It was created by missionaries to facilitate the spread of religion and education, and today tens of thousands of Canadians still regard Cree as their mother tongue.

![Cree syllabic text on street sign, middle part](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/DB2F2616-0A50-492B-A2DA-5619F0FA8173_1_105_c.jpeg)

## 🇨🇦Montreal & Quebec City

During my summer internship, a colleague strongly recommended me to come to Montreal. Indeed, this is a city that brings strong feelings when you first see it: the French street scene, the style of the old port, modern architecture and avant-garde art blend here. It has the efficiency of a North American city while retaining a French leisurely rhythm.

![Montreal was once the largest city in Canada, and its rise was closely related to its status as a shipping hub on the St. Lawrence River and the Great Lakes. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251020235021.png)

### Montreal: Trade, Conflict and Peace
![Montreal’s Notre-Dame Cathedral is a masterpiece of neo-Gothic architecture in the 19th century. Its blue-gold ceiling is extremely visually impactful. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/DCE9A8E3-B001-49F2-8AC3-C9BBACF92026_1_105_c.jpeg)

Contrary to popular belief, Montreal only really developed after the British took over. During the French rule, Quebec's political core was Quebec City, and Montreal was just a small trading post.

In the early 17th century, a small group of French settlers came here and had constant conflicts with the indigenous people. Due to the limited support of the French royal family, the colonists did not have the upper hand militarily. In the end, it was the indigenous people who took the initiative to sign a peace treaty to promote trade. The Great Peace of Montreal signed in 1701 was a rare, relatively equal treaty in North American history that brought decades of peace to the region. Later, diseases brought by Europe led to a sharp decline in the indigenous population, and French colonists were able to gradually expand.

![Exhibition at the Montreal Museum of Archeology and History, showing the diversity of the origins of the inhabitants. The museum was built on the remains of a 17th-century French settlement. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251103165908.png)

This history also explains why I see almost no “Land Acknowledgment” (Land Acknowledgment) for Aboriginal people in Quebec like other parts of Canada. The descendants of the French colonists here believe that they initially focused on trade and did not occupy land on a large scale. Instead, they were later conquered by the British and became the "governed" themselves.

![Overlooking downtown Montreal from Mount Royal. The park was planned by Olmsted, one of the designers of New York's Central Park. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/041AC875-BDD9-4B28-96C8-F2C91D9AD0CF_1_105_c.jpeg)

### Quebec’s “Great Darkness” and “Quiet Revolution”
Before the mid-20th century, Quebec was a fairly conservative, almost "theocratic" society. This period was later called the "Grande Noirceur" (Grande Noirceur). The conservative leader [Maurice Duplessis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Duplessis) (Maurice Duplessis), who came to power in 1936, maintained a society deeply dominated by the Catholic Church in the name of "protecting French culture." Education, medical care, and labor unions were all affected by it.

It was not until the death of Du Plessis in the 1960s that Quebec ushered in the "Révolution tranquille" - a smooth modern transition. The government took back its dominance in education and medical care, weakened the influence of the church, promoted industrialization and public services, and established a secular education system. It was during this period that Quebec's cultural confidence and identity today were established.

![Mary, Queen of the World Cathedral (Mary, Queen of the World Cathedral), coincides with the believers celebrating mass. ](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/D04275DC-F2D6-4EE2-8327-F820C3E01238_1_105_c.jpeg)

### Observations on the French language, colonization and multiculturalism
I have never quite understood why Canada attaches so much importance to French. In fact, the prototype of this country was born in the French colony. When the British took control of it in 1763, there was already a rich French community. The coexistence and competition between the British and French constitute Canada's national narrative of "two civilizations co-governing", and also extends to today's institutional guarantee of multiculturalism.

Preserving the French language is not only for cultural diversity, but also to maintain this historical narrative. Otherwise, Canada would be culturally almost indistinguishable from the United States. This also explains why the government insists on bringing in French-speaking immigrants through various programs, even if many people only see it as a shortcut to immigration. The political significance behind this is far greater than economic considerations. ~~Just like the CCP must need cross-strait reunification~~

Of course, this policy also brings practical contradictions. Quebec's language bill requires that commercial signs must be mainly in French. The most famous example is KFC being forced to change its name to PFK (Poulet Frit Kentucky) locally. The tension between the protection of the French language and economic openness is an issue that Quebec and even Canada as a whole continue to face.

In addition, unlike Spanish colonial rule, the relationship between the indigenous peoples of North America and the colonists was not simply one of invasion and invasion. In fact, it had a rich history of interaction and integration. These relationships were often driven by trade, geopolitics and tribal feuds, and were fraught with complex alliances.

For example, in the early 17th century, the French explorer Samuel formed a military alliance with indigenous tribes such as the Huron-Wendat and Algonquin in order to consolidate the lucrative fur trade. The French used muskets to help them fight against their traditional enemy, the powerful Iroquois Confederacy. In response, the Iroquois League turned to the Dutch, and then allied with the British, forming a confrontation that lasted for more than a century.

These alliances are not static. Aboriginal tribes are political entities with a high degree of autonomy. They choose which European power to cooperate with according to their own interests. This complex interaction constitutes an equally important narrative thread in North American colonial history.

After five days in Quebec, I flew to Ottawa, the capital of Canada. Located on the border of Ontario and Quebec, it was chosen as the capital in the mid-19th century because of its geographical neutrality, in order to reconcile the competition between the two major cities of Toronto and Montreal at the time.

As soon as you landed, the atmosphere of Ottawa's political center hit you: wide streets, neat buildings, and government agencies everywhere. This sense of order is also reflected in public security. ### Colonization, Civilization and Dehumanization

This is my first time in the Caribbean. Today, Puerto Rico's per capita GDP is about half that of the continental United States, and its living standards far exceed those of most Latin American countries, but the gap is still significant.

As a former Spanish colony, this also triggered my thinking about a phenomenon: Why do most of the former Spanish colonies (Latin American countries) struggle on the road to modernization and often fall into structural imbalances; while many former British colonies (such as the United States, Canada, Australia, and Singapore), despite having the same brutal and bloody history, have successfully established relatively stable modern countries? Some people say this is because they slaughtered all the indigenous people, but Spain slaughtered them more ruthlessly, but did not establish a better model.

Historians often compare two different colonial models: Spanish colonial rule was more of a "resource-extraction type". It established a strict social hierarchy and highly centralized power, aiming to transport the wealth of the New World back to the mother country. The institutional legacy left behind was often oligarchic politics and economic dependence;

British colonization, especially in North America, focused more on "settler society" and transplanted a whole set of systems including the common law system, private property rights, and parliamentary systems, laying the foundation for the subsequent development of capitalism.

Of course, history is far more complex than this division. My upbringing had accustomed me to view the colonizers as mere oppressors—even, as a completely dehumanized, brutal “monster.” But now I gradually feel that simply labeling individuals in history as "evil" actually falls into another kind of ideological laziness, which is a kind of reverse "dehumanization". This perspective ignores the huge power of institutions, environment and trends of the times to shape people.

Ironically, the reason why the colonists were able to commit all kinds of atrocities was precisely due to their concept of "dehumanization" of the colonial natives - viewing them as backward, ignorant, and "others" who could be manipulated at will. Separating the other party from the category of "human" makes exploitation and slavery feel at ease. This is also the source of many historical tragedies.

Of course, the concepts and behaviors we take for granted today may also have some blind spots and "dehumanization" that we are not aware of in the eyes of people a few hundred years from now.

At the same time, the flights before and after this are the highest class in the economy class (I thought it was business class at first...but it’s not bad), and it is also the highest specification seat I have ever flown🤣

In the evening, I urgently booked a hostel called HI NYC Hostel for $65 per night - the cheapest accommodation in New York. I discovered that the hostel I stayed in Chicago a few days ago was also called HI Chicago Hostel - it turned out to be part of the same non-profit youth hostel chain called HI USA, which was interesting.

It was already one o'clock in the morning when I arrived. After making up my clothes, I lay on the bed with the sound of the air conditioner roaring like the Titanic. I felt like a Chinese laborer who came to the United States a hundred years ago🤣

The history of New York needs no introduction. It is exactly what all movies and TV shows depict: busy, crowded, diverse and exciting. You can hear languages ​​from all over the world on the streets, like a miniature Earth. Everything here unabashedly demonstrates the purest and most original vitality of capitalism😆

Before returning to the youth hostel, I took the subway to Times Square.

In 2017, I came to the United States for two months as a high school exchange student. At that time, a group of us chartered a bus from Boston to travel through the eastern United States. In order to avoid being discovered by the Education Bureau, we did not even dare to post photos online🤣 (However, the project was canceled the next year, unfortunately), so we did not leave any records. I only remember that it was a cloudy noon with light rain. The 15-year-old me and my friends were wandering aimlessly on the streets of New York.

When we walked to Times Square, I was completely shocked by the sight before me: the huge billboards were flashing with colorful neon, changing colors in the fog after the rain, it was extremely cyberpunk. The streets are full of people and traffic. At that time, I was ignorant of the world, never thought about the future, and never cared about the current situation. Good old days🫠.
![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/53C6D4C0-C8AE-4E8B-86ED-1F65E228AE4B_1_105_c.jpeg)

Tonight, eight years later, I stand here again, surrounded by traffic and people. The freshness and shock of the year have faded away, replaced by an incredible feeling of intertwining time and space. I never thought I would come back here again, the world has changed so much in just a few short years.
