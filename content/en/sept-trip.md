The day after I finished my internship, I set out to travel to Eastern Canada.

Halifax and St. John's can be said to be the two most core cities in Canada before the British took over the French territory in 1763 - one is a military town and the other is a fishing center.

![The occupation of North America from 1754 to 1763](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251002163948.png)


Before getting to the main topic, it is necessary to briefly add the history of the confrontation between Britain and France in North America: The Seven Years' War (1756-1763) is called the "French and Indian War" in North America. It was actually a decisive battle between the two empires of Britain and France competing for North American hegemony. During the war, British forces not only conquered French Canada but also achieved important victories in India and the Caribbean. The Treaty of Paris signed on February 10, 1763 officially ended the war - France ceded all North American territory east of the Mississippi River (except New Orleans) to Britain, and Spain ceded Florida to Britain in exchange for Cuba.       

## Halifax

Halifax is the provincial capital of Nova Scotia. The name Nova Scotia is quite interesting - it is Latin and means "New Scotland". This land was originally the homeland of the indigenous people [Mi'kmaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%27kmaq), who called it Mi'kma'ki. After the French came, they called it [Acadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia). It was not until 1621 that the King of Scotland granted this land to the Scottish poet and explorer Sir William Alexander. In order to commemorate his monarch, this literary knight chose to name this land Nova Scotia in Latin. Although Scotland's colonization attempt lasted only three years between 1629 and 1632 and failed, this literary Latin name remained.

![BFEB8A10-0B1A-49F7-B4CB-E0115B85D3D4_1_105_c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/BFEB8A10-0B1A-49F7-B4CB-E0115B85D3D4_1_105_c.jpeg)


After leaving the airport, take the bus that runs every hour to the youth hostel in the city center. The first time I stayed in a youth hostel in North America, I discovered that locks are generally not provided here in North America, so I bought one at the front desk for $6.

![Still made in China](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20250917175512.png)

Canada's eastern Atlantic provinces have long faced labor outmigration, and Halifax is no exception. Like most immigrant cities, Halifax today is hardly a distinctive tourist city.

However, historically this was the port city with the largest passenger flow in Canada, especially after World War II. As the main port of entry for European immigrants, Halifax's Pier 21 port receives millions of migrants. An office building for immigration procedures next to the port is currently transformed into the Canadian National Immigration Museum (https://pier21.ca/), which preserves the waiting hall, archives and a large number of immigration story exhibitions at that time. New immigrants have to go through a series of procedures here until they finally know whether they can stay as residents. It is said that one fifth of Canada's residents now have direct or indirect connections with this port.

![Pier21 at 1934](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20250918150245.png)

![The place where the results of the immigration process are announced](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/29776E79-E883-4A58-AFA1-993A3FFD8DCF_1_105_c.jpeg)


Then went to the Maritime Museum of the Atlantic. Most cities in eastern Canada are highly related to the North Atlantic maritime affairs during World War I and World War II.

![Performance outside the Maritime Museum](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/4B444A6E-82E3-4BF5-9AF0-44EBB5F14A88_1_105_c.jpeg)

After the Titanic crashed, Halifax was the only large port nearby. The main rescue team set out from here, and more than a hundred unclaimed bodies were buried. The museum records some stories from that time.

It also records the port explosion that occurred in 1917. During World War I, the French cargo ship Montblanc loaded with ammunition collided with the Norwegian rescue ship Imo due to a series of geographical and man-made reasons, triggering the largest non-nuclear explosion in human history so far. The explosion killed about 2,000 people and injured 9,000 people. Almost every family was affected and became an important local collective memory.


![Hafa Military Fortress](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/9251DBD8-4660-467B-A33B-B9C043585FA3_1_105_c.jpeg)

The next day, we went to a star-shaped fortress built by the British army on the high ground in the local city center. There were performances every hour for the changing of the guard at that time. In addition to the introduction to the fortress at that time, there is also an Army Museum inside the castle that tells the story of World War I. It has very rich content and exhibits, describing the composition, strength, and battles of the Canadian Army at that time.

## St. Johns

I only stayed in Hafa for two days, and then headed to St. John's - the easternmost and oldest city in North America, which was also the purpose of my original trip.

Compared to Halifax, the history of St. John's and the entire island of Newfoundland is much more interesting, giving me a completely different feeling compared to other North American cities.    

![Newfoundland was still an independent dominion during World War I and sent separate regiments to participate in the European battlefield. At the Battle of Beaumont-Hamel in the Battle of the Somme in 1916, the Newfoundland Regiment was almost wiped out: of the more than 800 men who participated in the battle, only about 68 were able to answer the roll call that day. This tragic loss left few Newfoundland families completely unscathed. The First World War therefore became one of the heaviest collective memories in Newfoundland, profoundly affecting the entire society. The museum I visited later also had a lot of related content and stories](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/FBF4CAE2-9C32-4BB3-BEE5-C48B7F48109C_1_105_c.jpeg)

Newfoundland was named after the explorer John Cabot discovered the land in 1497 - "Newly Discovered Land". Although the Vikings briefly settled here as early as around 1000 AD (in the northernmost part of Newfoundland, the thatched houses of the Vikings at that time are preserved)

Interestingly, Newfoundland has maintained a high degree of autonomy for a long time in history:
- 16th-17th centuries: As one of the earliest British overseas colonies, living on fishing
- 1825: Officially became a British crown colony
- 1907: The status was upgraded to a "Dominion", alongside Canada, Australia, and New Zealand at the time. They were all "quasi-independent countries" under the British royal family.
- 1934: Voluntarily relinquishes its status due to financial collapse caused by the Great Depression (the only dominion in history to relinquish its status)
- 1949: After a referendum, a slim majority of 52% decided to join Canada and become the tenth province.

![The rooms, a local comprehensive museum, covers the history, nature, culture, art, etc. of Newfoundland and Labrador. The construction is quite high-quality](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/EC0D7F69-62B0-425C-8A3F-F61D3C457A01_1_105_c.jpeg)

Historically, Newfoundland has always relied on fishing as its core industry before the overfishing of cod in modern times, which has also created a unique local port culture: small fishing villages, colorful row houses and flocks of seabirds can be seen everywhere. Coupled with the unique climate of the Atlantic Ocean, it creates a feeling of wind and sea erosion and a world on the edge, similar to the "end of the world".    
![The colorful row houses where residents live, the local tour guide said that they are painted every ten years, which is both a long-standing tradition and for tourism promotion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/77B3897D-5EEA-450A-9EB4-7247576E8288_1_105_c.jpeg)

Since I don’t have a car and taking a taxi is expensive, I also signed up for a small guided tour led by a local Newfoundlander. Except for me and another Bangladeshi girl who just graduated from Toronto (also studied CS), the remaining four are all grandmothers from Australia or other places in Canada.

We went to Cape Spear, the easternmost point of the North American continent, which was originally used as an outpost to monitor the North Atlantic during World War I.      

![The easternmost point of the North American continent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/756B8653-E2B5-4DD2-B66C-0FF479459A08_1_105_c.jpeg)

Then we went to Signal Hill, which overlooks the entire town of St. Johns. It is called Signal Hill because it was the location where Marconi conducted his first transatlantic radio transmission experiment in 1901 (although there is controversy). The experiment successfully received the Morse code letter "S" sent from Cornwall, England. The invention of radio also had a huge impact on the Titanic rescue more than a decade later.
![Overlooking the entire St. Johns from Signal Hill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/BD721F66-0E4B-4FAD-A7C1-D206936776BF_1_105_c.jpeg)

![Parliament that took more than a hundred years in the 19th and 20th centuries](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/41CAFCF0-6625-48DF-A622-688E814FCE78_1_105_c.jpeg)

Due to the low degree of commercial development, many local specialty shops and restaurants have been preserved. In addition to trying the famous cod (a ban was issued in 1992 due to overfishing, the cod population collapsed and has not recovered to this day, and all we eat now are artificially farmed), we also ate at local Filipino and Ethiopian restaurants with high reviews. It is an interesting experience to taste food from all over the world in the easternmost part of North America.

Seattle is better known for its industry, pop culture, and technology scene—it’s the birthplace of Boeing and the headquarters of Amazon and Microsoft. The city's name "Seattle" comes from Chief Seattle, a respected local aboriginal chief.

Historically, the Great Fire of 1889 destroyed nearly the entire downtown community. Despite the scale of the disaster, fortunately there were few casualties. After the fire, the city was reorganized: streets were widened, buildings were generally converted to masonry, and better sewer and electrical systems were established. Today, tourists still have the ruins of the old city that were buried in the city center, but they didn't go to see them because of limited time.    

![Space Needle](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/CF44DCFD-2C2C-4E7B-987A-6BE9F5C1E05E_1_105_c.jpeg)

In fact, many cities in North America experienced similar disasters around the 19th century - the port explosion in Halifax, the 1892 fire in St. John's, and even Vancouver had a serious fire in 1886 that razed the city to the ground (the magician who performed at a magic show in Vancouver also mentioned this incident, which was very interesting).    

Although traumatic at the time, these events often serve as opportunities for urban reconstruction. It is in the wake of these disasters that many cities have the opportunity to plan streets, infrastructure and public services in a more modern way. It can be said that these accidents have to a certain extent shaped the seemingly more organized and convenient layout of North American cities today.

## Honolulu

My first impression of Honolulu was the description of this "empty land of consumerism" in "Wu Wu Wu" I read in high school. However, because the writing was so beautiful, I have been inexplicably yearning for Waikiki for a long time. After coming here, I feel that consumerism is still too far behind Tokyo🤣

I have been to the Big Island of Hawaii before in 2022, and the first feeling I felt when I got off the plane this time was the unique "transparency" of the Pacific islands - exactly the same as three years ago. The sky and sea are so clear that there is almost no layering. The picture seen by the naked eye is like applying a dehazing filter to the photo.

Honolulu is today the political and economic center of Hawaii and one of the most popular overseas vacation destinations for Japanese tourists. This is no accident: from the late 19th century to the early 20th century, Hawaii attracted a large number of Japanese labor immigrants. Before World War II, Japanese had become one of the largest ethnic groups in Hawaii. Because of this, Honolulu’s streets and neighborhoods have a strong Japanese imprint.

![Very Japanese-style residential building](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/C92962A4-2300-409E-A4BD-56B213323CF9_1_105_c.jpeg)

You can see Japanese-style buildings and restaurants everywhere when you walk in the city. Many restaurants adopt an ordering model similar to that of Japanese fast food: order at the counter, food is served quickly, and no tip is required—relatively rare elsewhere in North America. There are also a significant number of local Japanese tourists and residents, and I feel like about three-quarters of the Asian faces in Honolulu speak Japanese. Even in ordinary residential buildings, the architectural appearance and community atmosphere are often reminiscent of Japanese cities.

## Point Roberts
After dinner with friends on the last night of September, we decided on a whim to go to a place we had long wanted to visit but had never been to - Point Roberts. As early as July during my internship, I was curious about this peculiar land, but at that time my friends were not able to rent a car, and I had not yet learned to drive. The inconvenient transportation put the plan on hold.
![Point Roberts location on the map](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251002152333.png)

Point Roberts is a geographical wonder and a historical legacy. It belongs to the state of Washington in the United States, but it is an enclave completely separated from the mainland United States. This land is located at the southernmost tip of the Tsawwassen Peninsula on the south side of Vancouver (this is still an area with a high degree of indigenous autonomy). Because the British and American borders were simply cut along the 49th parallel in the 19th century when delimiting national borders, the tip of this peninsula was assigned to the United States. There are about 2,000 residents, similar to an American suburban town.

Therefore, whether you are a local resident or a visitor like us, if you want to travel to Point Roberts from the mainland United States, you must first enter Canada and then re-enter the United States - cross the border.

We drove in late at night and, not knowing the procedure, mistakenly thought we needed to get out of the car, which immediately resulted in a series of impatient complaints from the border officer. While checking our documents, he muttered and asked us, "What can we see if we come here for sightseeing in the middle of the night?" - one thing was true and the other was correct, but he still let us in.
