It’s the end of the year, so I’m just writing a little something.

I really put a lot into photography this year. Sometimes I feel like, if only I could have improved a bit faster.

Looking at the photos I took in the first half of the year and the ones from the second half, even I find it hard to believe they were taken by the same person.

In the first half of the year, I did a lot of output and only a little input. In the second half, I did a lot of input and only a little output (except for December, when I got absolutely crushed by deadlines and absurdly hard major courses, so I just gave up and slacked off).

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203054.png)

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203146.png)

Apart from making some progress in aesthetics, technique, and my understanding of composition and imagery, the thing that made me happiest this year was that I more or less figured out two questions:

1. Why I do photography
2. What kind of photos I want to take

Before I understood those two questions, all of my photos felt like they lacked emotion. All I obsessed over was composition, structure, color grading — all those basic and honestly kind of boring things.

Last year, the greatest pleasure photography gave me came from the result after the shoot. But this year, the feeling became more like this:
seeing a certain scene, feeling something inside me get stirred, not really being able to explain it, just knowing that I *need* to press the shutter. That split second of “being moved” is what matters now.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203221.png)

Looking back at what I shot back then, it feels kind of boring now haha.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203256.png)

![I will always love the light in libraries.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203316.png)

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203334.png)

I don’t know if other photographers feel this too when they shoot. I’ve always felt that the people who are *really* amazing at photography must also be the ones who are best at controlling when that moment happens. And moments like that usually require sensitivity, subtle observation, endless aesthetic accumulation, and maybe some talent too.

Sometimes I even have this kind of anti-intellectual thought:
what if photography itself is actually not all that mysterious, and it’s just that a small group of people simply have insanely good taste — and are good at using unusual images to make unusual expressions?

They capture the image that appears in that “trigger” moment, and then express it in a way ordinary people can understand. And that’s how composition, structure, emotion, and all those things come into being. Art is sometimes just that kind of mystical, hard-to-explain thing.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203357.png)

2021 was also my first year getting into portrait photography. I spent the whole spring shooting on my own and created plenty of black history.

![Everything I shot was way too weird hahaha.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203416.png)

At that time I was still in a state of understanding basically nothing (well, I still kind of am hahaha). The biggest problem was that I had no idea how those really good photographers actually took photos, no idea how I was supposed to think, and no confidence either. So I felt pretty lost.

Later I signed up for online courses by J God and Jiuji. I got a lot out of both, and they opened up many new ways of thinking for me.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203503.png)

I’m really, really grateful for that.

After coming back from Xiamen, I felt even more strongly that Japanese-style photography fits my aesthetic the best, so I decided that from now on I’d mainly focus on that style.

Around that time I looked at an enormous number of photos — so many that I even wrote a crawler to scrape several photographers’ Instagram photos hahaha.

Right now I’d still say I’m only at the beginner stage of portrait photography.

I can take photos that look decent enough visually and don’t have any obvious technical flaws, but deep down I also know that very few of those photos really contain my own expression. And after shooting like that for long enough, it gets kind of boring too.

I still need to think more, shoot more, and try more new things.

I need to look at and learn from many, many, many great photographers — not just their photos, but also their ideas and ways of thinking. And I’m grateful to every model I worked with this year.

## In Closing

![In Shanghai, autumn and winter only feel a little romantic during sunrise and sunset... I really miss Beijing’s autumn and winter.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130204151.png)

I remember very clearly that around this time last year, I had a very strong desire to *record this exact moment*. But this year, that feeling has faded quite a bit.

A lot of the time — especially around the end of the year — I feel like I’ve been in a state of complete numbness, both mentally and in my actions.

In the bigger picture, I still don’t know exactly what I want, but I also don’t feel all that lost. I feel like something about my current situation is off, but I don’t really know what needs to change. I’m not in much pain, but I’m also rarely happy.

In December, I often dreamed about August 2019 — the grade retreat before senior year, the late summer night when I saw fireflies for the first time, the night I snuck out with friends at midnight and lay on a wooden bridge in the middle of a lake counting stars.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130203912.png)

When I stare blankly at the sky, do I also carry the same confusion I feel right now?

Even so, there are still many moments when I feel content:
when I’m taking photos, when I’m traveling, when I’m messing around with my roommates, ~~when I’m eating with pretty older girls.~~

I’m most grateful for the people around me, my family, and especially my parents.

No matter what, they’ve always supported me and healed me, giving me the courage to keep doing what I believe is right.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130204013.png)
![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jiaaming/blogImage/main/pic/20251130204025.png)

No matter what, if I had to say what my biggest gain this year was, it would probably be that I’ve finally, finally managed to maintain emotional stability for a fairly long period of time.

All year long, I lived according to my own will. In a very idealistic way, I cast aside many of the troubles adults are supposed to worry about, and I think I more or less figured out the lifestyle that suits me best.

As for next year, I just hope I can put my energy into things that are more worth it — and I hope the pandemic disappears soon.
